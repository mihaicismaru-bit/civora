Date: 2026-09-22

## Benchmark set
Primary live benchmarks reviewed:
- The Wall Street Journal (US)
- The New York Times (US)
- Le Monde (France)
- Financial Times (UK)
- DER SPIEGEL (Germany)

Selection is for product/editorial benchmarking, not a claim that each is the highest-traffic news site in its country.

## Core findings

### 1. Homepage hierarchy
The strongest products do not present one undifferentiated reverse-chronological feed.
They combine:
1. a sharply ranked lead package;
2. supporting stories around the lead;
3. topical verticals;
4. editorial-product verticals such as analysis, opinion, explainers, investigations, interviews, live coverage, data/graphics and service journalism;
5. recurring utility/news products;
6. clear links to dedicated section or product landing pages.

WSJ/FT are especially disciplined around lead hierarchy and vertical packages.
NYT uses strong event/topic hubs and multiple storytelling forms around one major subject.
Le Monde makes format labels such as Décryptage, En graphiques, En cartes, Enquête and Synthèse reader-visible.
DER SPIEGEL exposes article type, byline, reading time and related-topic navigation very clearly.

### 2. The article is a product, not only a page
The benchmark sites change the grammar of the article depending on what the reader is consuming.

#### Straight news
- fact and consequence in the first paragraph;
- compact deck;
- visible timestamp;
- strong attribution;
- context follows the new fact, not before it;
- concise related-story links.

#### Explainer / analysis
- question or mechanism is explicit;
- reader gets the answer early;
- structured sections;
- numbers are interpreted, not merely reproduced;
- known / unknown / next steps are separated;
- maps, data, diagrams or documents are used when they materially improve understanding.

#### Fact-check / verification
- claim under examination is explicit;
- evidence chain is visible;
- conclusion is bounded by what evidence supports;
- uncertainty and source limitations are visible.

#### Investigation / dossier
- larger visual and headline scale;
- chronology/evidence/document trail;
- entities and money/decision chains;
- methodology and source transparency;
- right of reply where applicable;
- follow-up links to the dossier.

#### Interview
- clearly labelled as interview;
- subject and relevance established before Q&A;
- questions edited for coherence;
- no disguise of interview as reported straight news.

#### Opinion / commentary
- visually and semantically separated from reporting;
- named author;
- no ambiguity that interpretation belongs to the author/editorial board.

#### Live / rolling coverage
- dedicated event hub rather than repeated duplicate articles;
- newest verified update first;
- timestamp per update;
- key context persists separately from the update stream.

#### Service journalism
- answer-first structure;
- dates, locations, eligibility, prices, contacts and practical consequences surfaced early;
- minimal narrative overhead.

### 3. Headline + deck
Across the benchmark:
- headline carries the new fact or useful promise;
- deck answers why the story matters;
- headline and deck do different jobs;
- labels such as Analysis / Interview / Explainer / Investigation help set reader expectations;
- clickbait is unnecessary when specificity itself creates interest.

### 4. Reader trust signals
Repeated benchmark patterns:
- visible publication/update times;
- byline;
- clear format label;
- source/photo credits;
- related stories/topics;
- reading time or equivalent expectation-setting in some products;
- visible corrections/updates where applicable;
- strong separation of reporting, analysis and opinion.

### 5. Navigation model
The useful common denominator is a two-axis model:
- AXIS A — editorial products: latest, straight news, explainers, verification, dossier/investigation, guides/service;
- AXIS B — topics/geography: politics/administration, economy, safety, culture, sport, places/regions.

Do not use fake navigation destinations or empty SEO pages. A product or topic page should exist only when it has real content.

## VÂLCEA CLAR canonical application

### Homepage
Use:
1. one editorial lead;
2. 2–4 supporting stories;
3. current verified stream;
4. editorial-product modules;
5. topic/geography modules;
6. service blocks for traffic, utilities, events and deadlines;
7. dossier/follow-up modules when warranted;
8. no filler modules solely for symmetry.

### Article contracts

#### PE SCURT
Target: fast utility.
- 120–350 words unless the facts require more.
- first two sentences: what happened + who is affected.
- one compact context paragraph.
- source/date.
- what changes now, if relevant.

#### VÂLCEA AZI / straight news
Target: complete local news.
- new fact immediately.
- deck explains significance.
- consequences and attribution early.
- context after the new fact.
- “what follows” near the end.

#### CLARIFICĂM
Target: mechanism/explanation.
- state the question/problem.
- answer early.
- sections based on reader questions.
- interpret numbers and documents.
- explicitly separate what is known, what is unclear and what follows.

#### VERIFICAT
Target: claim verification.
- claim;
- evidence;
- source quality;
- finding;
- caveat/limits.
Never use a stronger verdict than the evidence allows.

#### CE URMEAZĂ
Target: follow-up.
- current status;
- exact next milestone;
- who decides;
- deadline/date;
- unresolved dependency;
- what would count as a material change.

#### DOSAR
Target: durable context.
- thesis/question;
- timeline;
- document trail;
- people/institutions/companies;
- money/procurement/decision chain where relevant;
- related stories;
- open questions.

#### ANCHETĂ
DOSAR plus:
- methodology;
- evidence standard;
- right of reply;
- legal/reputational gate;
- transparent limits.

#### PROFIL / OAMENI
- why this person matters locally;
- verified biography/context;
- contribution/controversy only where relevant and sourced;
- human detail without private-life intrusion.

#### INTERVIU
When used:
- clearly labelled INTERVIU;
- short scene/context introduction;
- Q&A structure;
- identify editing/translation when material.

#### UNDE IEȘIM / service
- date/time;
- place;
- price/access/booking where verified;
- who organizes;
- what reader should know before going;
- source freshness.

#### PAMFLET / SATIRĂ
- unmistakable label;
- verified factual kernel;
- explicit separation between fact and exaggeration.

### Universal article header
Every article should expose, when available:
- editorial product label;
- topic/section;
- headline;
- deck;
- byline;
- publication time;
- updated time only when materially updated;
- estimated reading time;
- locality;
- image caption/credit/provenance.

### Universal article footer
- sources/documents;
- corrections/update note when applicable;
- related stories;
- related product/topic;
- next follow-up when relevant.

## Anti-copy rule
VÂLCEA CLAR adopts principles, not distinctive trade dress, wording, typography, icons, page composition or branded product names from benchmark publications.

## Acceptance
This research is considered applied only when:
- product labels drive distinct writing contracts;
- article pages visibly distinguish major product types;
- homepage supports importance + product + topic discovery;
- related stories and reading-time expectations exist;
- empty/fake nav destinations are absent;
- the hourly newsroom prompt enforces these contracts.
